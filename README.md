# Advanced Film Holder

This is a guide for 3D printing, assembly and using a continuous-feed film holder for Plustek OpticFilm 8100 and 8200 scanners.

## 3D Printing

I don’t have strong recommendations yet for printing these parts, so feel free to experiment. During development, I have been printing with the following parameters:

| Parameter | Used for development |
| ----: | :---- |
| Printer | Bambulabs X1C |
| Nozzle | 0.2 mm |
| Bed | Smooth PEI plate |
| Filament | Bambu PLA |
| Layer height | 0.06 \- 0.1 mm (High Quality setting) |

The individual parts are designed to be printed in specific orientations. Auto-orientation features of your slicer should figure it out correctly, but please double-check the screenshot below.

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b8c0f9ab-21cb-4b51-ad36-22f537edf54b)

## Assembly

1. Thoroughly clean up the parts.

2. Glue the two film holder parts together with super glue. Make sure not to get glue near the groove through which the film travels.  
   ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/161908ed-ef9d-40b5-a73c-7040650acce6)


3. Glue the two axle parts together with super glue.  
   ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a3652522-1090-456a-9217-71dffc5bd086)



4. Slide on the indexing ring. The small depression on the side should be facing outwards. There are some thin retention features on the inside of the ring. Be careful not to break them during assembly. Once the ring is on it should be able to rotate freely.  
   ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/708245ea-abc5-434b-8c8a-fb55c8ae5dba)



5. Screw in the indexing knob. It should be able to rotate with relatively little friction until it locks the indexing ring in place.  
   ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1bef9a58-1968-4857-9111-efcbad3647b9)



6. Mount the subassembly in the bracket.  
   ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/79a175bc-3e4c-4239-a1a0-4b5c62535056)



7. The bracket can now be clicked onto the film holder. This can be done in two orientations. It is up to you which orientation you prefer.  
   ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3f5bb241-2fc0-49b8-84e4-1662007f4fa6)



## Using the Film Holder

1. Slide the film holder into the scanner until it snaps into place.  
2. Make sure the indexing knob is loose and the indexing ring is in the upright position. This will allow you to move the film by rotating only the advance dial.  
3. Prepare a non-abrasive surface on which the unscanned section of your film can rest while scanning.  
4. Insert the start of your uncut roll of film into the film holder. See the image below for which direction the film should go through the film holder. It is technically possible to have it go in the other direction, but I strongly recommend *pulling* the film through the scanner. *Pushing* the film through has a higher chance of skipping a sprocket hole, which would misalign your frames.  
     ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/145c0064-3a14-4766-889c-8584759d1151)

5. Advance the film to where your second exposed frame is in the middle of the scanner. Now use the frame alignment notches to do an initial alignment of the film. The notches should align with the gaps between exposed frames. You might have to move the film holder to the left or right to properly see them.  
     ![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8ce95361-94ca-4ca9-97fd-ff4088a8e962)

6. After initial alignment using the notches, you can start a Prescan in the SilverFast scanning software. During the Prescan you can move the film back and forward to precisely align the frame. The last alignment correction you make should be done by rotating the advance dial in the same direction as you will do to advance the film during scanning. This will ensure to most precise alignment.  
7. Tighten the indexing knob to lock down the frame alignment. Tighten it properly so it won't shift during scanning.  
8. Reverse back one frame to scan the first frame on the roll. Then scan the entire roll frame by frame, each time rotating the advance dial one full rotation until it snaps into place.  
9. Take the opportunity to cut your roll to the lengths you need for storing. I recommend doing this while there is no scan in progress, as touching the film while scanning can result in distortions.

Check your scanned frames now and then to make sure nothing has misaligned or gone wrong. Even though there might be some small alignment differences between frames, it should remain consistent throughout the entire roll.

------

## More tips

- Ensure the film has completely dried before feeding it through the film holder. Wet film will lubricate the wheels causing them to slip.
